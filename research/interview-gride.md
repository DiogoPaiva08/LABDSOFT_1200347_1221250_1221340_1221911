# CampusFlow — User Research: Interview Guide

Part of issue [#8](../../issues/8) — User Research. Feeds `docs/03-user-research.md`.

**Goal:** validate or kill hypotheses H1–H3 from `docs/01-problem-opportunity.md`, and
surface anything the team hasn't thought of yet.

**Target:** 4–6 short interviews before drafting the survey — 2–3 students, 2–3 staff
from different POIs (canteen, bar, library).

**Length:** 10–15 minutes. Ask permission to take notes; no names in the write-up — use "Student A", "Canteen staff B", etc.

## For students / teaching staff (as users)

1. Walk me through the last time you went to [canteen/library/bar/academic services] — what made you
   decide to go at that moment?
2. Has there ever been a time you *didn't* go somewhere, or turned back, because you
   expected it to be crowded? What happened?
3. When you're deciding between two options (e.g. two canteens), how do you currently
   figure out which is less busy? (Probe: do you ask a friend, look at a window, just
   guess?)
4. What's more annoying — a long-but-predictable wait, or a short-but-uncertain one?
   Why?
5. If there were an app that showed live estimated wait times, would you check it
   before heading somewhere? What would make you *not* trust it?
6. Would you be willing to tap a button ("busy" / "not busy") when you arrive
   somewhere, if it helped others? What would stop you from doing that?

## For POI staff (canteen / bar / library)

1. Walk me through a typical day — when are your worst peaks, and are they
   predictable?
2. How do people currently react when it's crowded — do they leave, wait, complain?
3. If you had a screen or app showing live occupancy to visitors, would that help you
   or create more work? What would worry you about it?
4. Is there anything about how people queue or arrive that a purely digital system
   wouldn't be able to see?

---

## After each interview — write down immediately

- 2–3 direct quotes (anonymized) that best capture their experience
- Which hypothesis (H1 / H2 / H3) this supports, contradicts, or doesn't address
- One new thing you didn't expect
